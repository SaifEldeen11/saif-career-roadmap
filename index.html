CORE / BOTH PATHS

Swimming Academy Operations

The problem

A coordinator needs to schedule sessions, avoid overbooking, record attendance, and restrict access between admin, coach, and swimmer roles. Start with one workflow after interviewing a willing coach or academy coordinator. Until there are real users, call it a prototype.

Build the minimum dependable version

ASP.NET Core API, SQL Server, validation, role and resource authorization, migrations, integration tests, Docker, GitHub Actions, structured logs, a backup-and-restore drill, and a simple usable interface. Keep Redis only where a measured bottleneck justifies it.

Your definition of done

  • A new developer starts it from the README.
  • Tests reject overbooking and unauthorized access to another swimmer's data.
  • CI runs on a pull request; a reviewer comments on at least one change.
  • A live or recorded demo shows a complete workflow.
  • A benchmark states workload, hardware, sample size and latency; no invented improvement percentages.
  • A changelog documents a user-requested change or clearly labeled simulated requirement.
How to choose without splitting your effort

Spend two weeks on each path after the core deployment: one small prediction experiment, then one security assessment of your own app. Score enjoyment, ability to explain mistakes, finished artifacts, and fit with 20 recent target postings. Pick one primary specialty for the next 12 weeks. Keep the other to a small curiosity session. Do not make AI-for-cybersecurity your first project unless you can already evaluate both systems separately.

Current official guidance allows qualifying international students up to 140 full or 280 half working days yearly, with alternative counting rules and student-job exceptions. COMPETITIVE STANDARDDEPTH, REVIEW, AND DELIVERY

Stand out under scrutiny.

Explore the competitive engineering standard

The job market can be competitive even when experienced IT specialists are in demand. Do not interpret a shortage as easy entry for juniors. Your strongest differentiator is evidence that you can own a small feature from requirement to tested release, receive criticism, and correct it. The standards below are stretch targets for this roadmap, not universal hiring criteria.

Go beyond a CRUD demo

  • Concurrency: two users attempt the last available place. Demonstrate why a transaction or concurrency strategy prevents overbooking; a normal happy-path test is insufficient.
  • Reliability: retrying a booking must not create duplicates. Simulate a dependency failure and explain error handling, recovery and logs.
  • Performance: find a slow query with measurements, inspect the query plan, fix the cause, and rerun the same workload. Explain when caching helps and when stale data hurts.
  • Security: test object-level authorization and secret handling. Map selected checks to OWASP ASVS; do not claim full compliance from a few tests. [8]
  • Operation: deploy once, document configuration, perform a restore drill and explain how to roll back a release. Learn one hosting platform only after the local system works.

Pass an outside review

Ask a willing experienced developer, mentor or university peer to review one pull request and your case study. Ask where they would hesitate to approve it. Record the issues and your corrections. If nobody is available, use a peer review now and keep seeking a stronger review; AI feedback alone is not independent professional validation.

Prepare a ten-minute demonstration: requirement → design → failure test → fix → measured outcome → limitations. Then answer “Why this architecture?”, “What breaks at ten times the load?”, “How did you validate the result?” and “What would you simplify?” without memorized claims.

Interview gate: debug unfamiliar code, write a SQL join and discuss indexing, reason about a transaction, solve a modest array/map problem with complexity, and explain your own code without generated answers.

Raise the bar in your chosen specialty

AI: use a chronological holdout, a reproducible baseline, error analysis by meaningful slices, and a model/version record. Demonstrate behavior on missing or shifted inputs. If you later add retrieval or an LLM, compare it against a simpler solution using a fixed evaluation set, latency, cost and failure cases. A chat interface alone is weak evidence.

Cybersecurity: reproduce findings in an authorized environment, distinguish exploitability from scanner severity, prioritize by business impact, implement fixes, and show regression tests. Include an incident-style log investigation and explain false positives. A lab completion total alone is weak evidence.

Your monthly market calibration

Maintain a rolling sample of 20 recent employer listings across your realistic locations: roughly half adjacent entry roles and half your chosen specialty. Record repeated must-have skills, language, semester/enrollment requirements and eligibility. This is a small decision aid, not a labor-market estimate. Each month pick the most repeated missing capability you can demonstrate through your existing project. Avoid adding a new technology for one unusual posting. Review the plan after interview feedback, not only after course completion.

What “top skills” means at your stage. Reliable fundamentals plus unusually clear evidence for a junior. Do not try to become expert in .NET, AI, cloud, DevOps and cybersecurity simultaneously. Finish the core system, choose one specialty, and use teamwork and real feedback to deepen it. Keep APs, German and admission readiness protected.

06 / GETTING STARTEDSTART SMALL. FINISH SOMETHING.

Your starting moves.

Choose your first actions

Four concrete actions

  1. Audit the CV: label projects accurately; attach repository links; remove claims without evidence.
  2. Run the academy project: list three defects or missing acceptance criteria. Pick one booking workflow.
  3. Create one reviewed change: write a failing test, fix the behavior, document it in a pull request; invite a willing peer or mentor to review.
  4. Start German: assess your starting level, choose one structured course, and schedule two speaking sessions.

A pace that respects school

During AP and admissions periods, budget 5–7 technical hours per week: 3–4 building, 1 fundamentals/interview practice, and 1–2 review or collaboration. German is a separate short daily habit. In quieter weeks, increase technical work to 8–10 hours if sustainable. In exam weeks, reduce it to maintenance.

Practice arrays/maps, complexity, SQL joins and transactions, HTTP, debugging, and explaining tradeoffs. Two thoughtful coding problems per week are enough to maintain a habit; a problem count is not your main result. Keep gym, swimming and recovery in the schedule.

Budget rule: start with free docs and labs, local Docker/WSL and small CPU-friendly models. Buy neither a cloud certificate nor GPU access unless a target role or measurable project need justifies it.
